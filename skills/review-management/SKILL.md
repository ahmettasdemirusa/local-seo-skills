# Review Management

Reviews feed the **prominence** part of Google's local ranking. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can improve a business's local ranking (https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). They are also the part of local SEO with the most legal risk.

Two rulebooks apply. Where they differ, **follow the stricter one**:

- **Google's contribution policy** for Maps and Business Profiles: https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114
- **The US FTC Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials** (16 CFR Part 465), in effect since October 21, 2024:
  - https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers
  - Courts can impose civil penalties for knowing violations; the FTC cited up to $53,088 per violation in December 2025.

## Allowed

- **Asking every customer** for an honest review.
- **Sharing the review link or QR code.**
  - Find it in the Business Profile under Read reviews → Get more reviews (https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815).
  - QR codes can only be generated in a desktop browser.
  - Good places: receipts, invoices, thank-you emails, the end of a support chat, an in-store sign.
- **Replying** to reviews, positive and negative. The profile must be verified.
- **Contacting an unhappy reviewer** to fix the problem, and **asking a satisfied customer to update** their review. The FTC says organizing reviews is not suppression.
- **Flagging** reviews that break Google's policy: spam, conflicts of interest, off-topic or hateful content.

## Not allowed

| Practice | Google | FTC rule (US) |
|---|---|---|
| **Any incentive** for posting, changing or removing a review, such as discounts, free items or entries into a draw | Banned | Banned when the incentive depends on the review's sentiment, even an implied one. The FTC's example: "Tell us how much you loved your visit… and get a $5 coupon". |
| **Review gating:** asking only happy customers, or sending unhappy ones to a private form first | Banned | Not named in the rule, but the FTC says it could violate the FTC Act |
| **Pressuring customers,** e.g. making them review on the premises or asking for specific wording | Banned | — |
| **Staff quotas,** or asking reviewers to mention an employee by name | Banned | — |
| **Reviews by employees, contractors, owners or relatives** of the business | Removed as a conflict of interest | Insider reviews must clearly disclose the connection |
| **Fake reviews,** including AI-generated ones or reviews from people with no real experience | Banned | Banned to write, buy, sell or knowingly publish |
| **Buying reviews** or using review farms | Banned | Banned |
| **Suppressing negative reviews** with baseless legal threats, intimidation or false accusations | — | Banned |
| **Buying followers or views** (fake social influence) | — | Banned |

Because Google bans **all** review incentives, advise **no incentives at all** for Google reviews, even where the FTC would allow an unconditional one.

**Penalties.** Google can block new reviews for a period, unpublish existing ones, and show consumers a warning that fake reviews were removed (https://support.google.com/business/answer/14114287). Enforcement also happens: in May 2026 the FTC and Illinois sued a company over thousands of fake local home-repair listings and fabricated five-star reviews.

If the user asks for a banned tactic, decline to build it. Explain the risk in one or two sentences and offer the compliant alternative.

## Review request templates

Keep requests short, neutral and sent to **every** customer. Never add "if you were happy" and never add a reward.

**SMS**
> Hi {first name}, thanks for choosing {business}. If you have a minute, we'd appreciate an honest review: {review link}

**Email**
> Subject: How did we do?
>
> Hi {first name},
>
> Thank you for {service/visit} on {date}. Reviews help other people in {city} find a business they can trust, and they help us improve.
>
> Would you share your experience? It takes about a minute: {review link}
>
> {name}, {business}

**Printed card or sign**
> Tell us how we did. Scan to review us on Google. [QR code]

## Replying to reviews

Reply to all of them, reasonably soon. Replies are public and are moderated before they appear, usually within minutes and sometimes longer.

**Positive review**
- Thank them by name and mention something specific they said.
- Keep it to one or two sentences.
- No promotional offers in the reply.

**Negative review**
1. Stay calm and professional. Never argue about details in public.
2. Acknowledge the experience and apologize for how it felt, without admitting facts you haven't verified.
3. Take it offline: "Please call {name} at {phone} so we can make this right."
4. Never reveal the customer's private information, such as their appointment, diagnosis or order details. That matters most for medical, legal and financial businesses.
5. Don't make personal attacks, and don't threaten legal action.

**Template**
> Hi {name}, thank you for telling us about this. We're sorry your {visit/service} didn't meet expectations. We'd like to understand what happened and put it right. Please contact {manager} at {phone or email}.

**Fake or policy-violating review:** flag it through the Business Profile, then post one short, factual public reply, e.g. "We have no record of this visit and have reported the review. If we're mistaken, please contact us at {phone}."

## Building a steady review flow

- **Ask at the moment of satisfaction:** right after the job is done, the table is paid, or the ticket is closed.
- **Build the request into the process** (receipt, invoice, follow-up email), so every customer is asked, not just the friendly ones.
- **Track the count and rating monthly against the top three competitors in the map pack.** Steady, recent reviews look natural. A sudden spike looks like manipulation.
